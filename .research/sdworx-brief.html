1. Welcome to the challenge

Every large organisation holds the combined knowledge of its people. That knowledge only creates value when others can use it with confidence. Too often, it is scattered, duplicated, outdated or confined to systems, teams and individual experience.

At this hackathon, your task is to explore how technology, design and human insight can turn that complexity into confidence. Build a focused proof of concept that could help employees find, understand and trust what they need.

2. Who is SD Worx?

SD Worx helps organisations across Europe manage HR, payroll and workforce operations, keeping people employed, paid and supported. The company has been doing this for more than 80 years. Its operations span Europe, with payroll reach in more than 100 countries. That scale creates extensive expertise and makes knowledge harder to navigate.

3. The hidden problem inside large organisations

Inside SD Worx, knowledge lives in:

Documents

Policies, manuals, procedures, checklists, analyses

Conversations

Emails, chats, Teams channels, shared files

Systems

Workflows, business applications, operational data

People

Knowledge held only by experts and experienced colleagues
Finding information is only the first step. A search can return ten answers, and an AI assistant can summarise them. The harder question is whether an answer is reliable, current and relevant to this customer, country or situation.

4. Why this matters

People across the organisation rely on knowledge and expertise every day to support customers, build products, improve services and make informed decisions. Uncertainty slows decisions, leads to repeated work and makes valuable expertise hard to reuse. As organisations grow, more knowledge becomes available, but it becomes harder to know what to trust.

This is fundamentally a trust problem. A useful solution should give people an answer and show why they can trust it, where uncertainty remains and who can help when documents are not enough.

5. Real stories, real friction

Scenario A: The urgent customer question

An employee needs an answer now. An AI assistant finds three documents: one recently updated, one without an owner, and one that may apply to another country. Then a colleague shares contradictory information from a Teams conversation. The employee has found information but still cannot act with confidence.

Scenario B: The inherited portfolio

A payroll consultant inherits a client portfolio. Years ago, the handover might have been one document and a one-hour conversation. Today, that knowledge may be spread across documents, chats, workflows, applications, business data and experts in different teams.

The information exists. Confidence does not come automatically.

6. Your challenge

Build a focused proof of concept that makes organisational knowledge easier to find, trust or share. Choose one meaningful problem. You do not need to solve everything.

The challenge centres on these questions:

  • What is reliable?
  • What is current?
  • What applies in this context?
  • Where are the gaps?
  • Who has relevant expertise?
  • Which answer should a person trust?

7. Inspiration areas

Trust

How can people tell whether information is relevant and reliable?

Capture

How can valuable knowledge be shared beyond inboxes, documents and isolated teams?

Detect

How can we identify conflicting, duplicated, missing or outdated knowledge?

Connect

How can people find the right expertise when documents are not enough?

Use these as inspiration, not as a checklist. Do not start with prescribed technology. Start with the moment of doubt, friction or uncertainty that you want to change.

8. Build something bold

Focus on one role, one workflow, one knowledge source or one trust signal. Show a concrete moment of doubt. Then show how your proof of concept helps someone move from "I found something" to "I understand why I can rely on it." A strong concept makes trust visible, explainable and useful.

Be curious and challenge assumptions. Combine technical ingenuity with a deep understanding of human behaviour. The strongest ideas won't hide complexity behind a black box.

Aikido security audit (required, 10%)

The Aikido AI Code Audit reasons about your code's actual logic and access patterns. It checks for:

  • Business logic flaws: ways to bypass or abuse the app's intended rules or workflows
  • IDOR: access to other users' data by manipulating IDs or references
  • Authentication: weaknesses in how users are verified
  • Authorization: weak permission checks that let users take actions or view data beyond their role

Process

  1. Go to app.aikido.dev/ai-pentests/discounts/hackathon-tectonic-aikido and click Continue with GitHub.
  2. Connect your hackathon repo.
  3. Run the AI Code Audit (credits provided). This is your baseline, so take a screenshot.
  4. Fix the issues and mark them as resolved.
  5. Your score is based on the remaining issues. Take the "after" screenshot.
Tip: role-based access matters because the challenge includes who can access which knowledge. Build in authentication and authorization from the start.
